Waiting for wind…

Wind is the Tân Sơn Nhất METAR, measured at 10 m about 7 km away. Wind at the flag, about 500 m up, is estimated from that 10 m reading with the power-law profile used for dense city centres (roughly 3× stronger). The flag matches the giant one on Çamlıca Hill, Istanbul: 1,000 m² (about 38.7 × 25.8 m, official 2:3 proportions) on a 110 m pole. It moves by a cloth simulation driven by that estimated wind.

MODEL PROVENANCE

City-wide streets.
Local detail on demand.

This static model covers the bounding rectangle of the supplied HCMC camera locations, with about 1 km of padding. It is not the full municipal administrative boundary.

The existing traffic application is separate and unchanged. This viewer has no traffic simulation or CCTV capture loop.

What comes from OpenStreetMap?

Street alignments, mapped building footprints, water and park polygons, and bridge/tunnel tags. Construction and proposed roads are excluded.

What is estimated?

Widths use a mapped width when available, otherwise lane counts or road-class defaults. Heights use mapped height, levels × 3.2 m, or a 10.5 m default. Bridge elevation uses 6 m per layer with schematic approaches. The ground is flat.

Missing buildings reflect map coverage, not necessarily empty land. The whole-city overview simplifies road lines; the detailed GLB tiles retain source centreline vertices. Individual tiles can be downloaded from the manifest and imported into 3D software in a common coordinate system.

Where do the aircraft come from?

Tân Sơn Nhất arrivals and departures are scraped from the airport's own live flight board at tia.vietnamairport.vn, refreshed a few minutes at a time, with the runway direction chosen from the airport's METAR wind. Aircraft are animated along schematic approach and climb paths at the board's scheduled or estimated time; no ADS-B receiver covers HCMC on the open aggregators, so positions are not tracked. When the live board can't be reached the layer shows a labelled sample cadence instead, never a stale flight presented as current. Runway geometry is from OurAirports.

What is Lightning?

A dramatisation of the same camera rain reports, not a lightning detection feed — none covering HCMC was found. About half of the flashes are in-cloud sheet lightning; the rest are branched channels to camera-reported Medium/Heavy rain, favouring the tallest nearby building (recovered from the persistent skyline model) so towers like Landmark 81 get struck far more than a low rooftop. Each flash is several return strokes down one channel. It is off by default and carries a flashing-images warning. Thunder sound is a second, independent toggle: it is synthesised from the bolt’s own shape and your viewpoint — each part of the channel is a different distance away, so the sound arrives spread out and rolls, reaches you only after its travel time, and cracks when close but only rumbles when far. No audio file is involved.

Why is there a sun or a moon?

Whichever is actually above the horizon right now, at its real position, computed from your clock and this city's coordinates — not scheduled or looped. The sun renders hazy: soft-edged and warm, brighter and less amber the higher it climbs, the way it actually looks through this city's humidity. The moon renders clear: a hard-edged disc showing its real current phase, which is why it looks close to full around Tết Trung Thu (Mid-Autumn) — that festival is timed to the full moon. The scene's main light follows whichever body is up, dimming and cooling at night.

The original traffic app, live feed settings and .env were not modified.
